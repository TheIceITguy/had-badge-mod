# v1.1.0

v1.1.0 fixes how the badge reaches the ICM-20948's magnetometer and adds two alternative sensors, but
the compass heading is still suppressed and this release does not change that. The magnetometer was
being read through I2C bypass mode, which reads a part's identity and control registers perfectly while
returning noise for its measurements, so three modules from two suppliers looked counterfeit when the
access path was at fault. That is corrected. What remains is roughly 200 to 400 µT of interference on
the badge itself against an earth field of 25 to 65 µT, which is a hardware problem, not a firmware
one. Read [What is still broken](#what-is-still-broken) before deciding whether to upgrade for the
compass. Everything else here is additive and safe.

No breaking changes. No setting defaults moved, and a badge upgrading from v1.0.0 behaves the same
unless you deliberately select a different magnetometer.

Previous release: v1.0.0.

## The magnetometer is now reached the way the manufacturer intended

The AK09916 magnetometer sits on a private I2C bus behind the ICM-20948, not on the badge's bus. Two
ways exist to reach it, and v1.0.0 used the wrong one.

Bypass mode connects that private bus to the host bus so the magnetometer looks like an ordinary second
device. It is the simpler path and it is a trap. On this badge it read the identity register, accepted a
soft reset, reported its configuration back correctly, and returned measurements that were noise. Counts
came back six to eleven thousand out with no visible response to the magnetometer's own self-test coil.

v1.1.0 uses the ICM's auxiliary I2C master instead, which is what every working driver for this package
does. One channel performs single configuration transactions; another is armed as a standing order that
re-reads the nine data registers on a schedule and leaves them in the ICM's shadow registers, so a
sample is one ordinary read with every byte from the same transaction. Over that path the self-test
shows a real coil response and the counts drop by a factor of five.

A second bug went with it. The auxiliary master's repeat rate was left at its reset value, which polls a
100 Hz magnetometer at 1.1 kHz, and every one of those reads touches the register that tells the
magnetometer its data has been consumed. Measured spread across that setting on real hardware: 440 µT at
1.1 kHz against 200 µT at 69 Hz, so over-polling was corrupting about half the reading.

## Two alternative magnetometers, selectable in Settings

`mag_source` chooses where the heading comes from. The default is unchanged, so nothing moves unless you
select something else, and the service reads it at boot, so a change needs a restart.

| `mag_source` | Part | What the badge does |
|--------------|------|---------------------|
| `imu` (default) | AK09916 inside the ICM-20948 | Field from the ICM, fused with its accelerometer |
| `qmc5883l` | Module at `0x0D` | Field from the module, fused with the ICM's accelerometer |
| `bno055` | Module at `0x28` | The part reports a finished heading; the badge's tilt maths, sweep and self-test sit idle |

Neither new driver has met its hardware. Both compile and the host suite passes, but treat first-boot
behaviour as unproven, and expect a rotated or mirrored heading until the axis mapping is set for however
you mount the module. That is documented, not a bug to chase.

The QMC5883L defaults follow what was measured on the badge rather than the usual examples: ±8 gauss
rather than ±2, because ±2 is 200 µT full scale and would clip on this badge's interference alone and
look like a broken sensor, and maximum oversampling for the narrowest internal filter. That filter is
the reason the part is worth trying at all, since it works inside the sensor where averaging in firmware
cannot reach.

A BNO055 is different in kind. It fuses its own heading, using its magnetometer only as a slow drift
reference while its gyroscope carries short-term motion, so a noisy field still yields a stable bearing.
It also needs no ICM, having its own accelerometer.

Nothing falls back. If the selected module does not answer, the badge boots with no heading and says so,
rather than reverting to the AK09916 and presenting the fault you are escaping as the new module's.

## A magnetometer self-test on F3 in Diagnostics

The AK09916 can measure a field generated by a coil on its own die, so the datasheet fixes the counts a
healthy part returns. F3 runs it five times and reports the verdict, the tally and the last counts.

Read it as the coil field plus the ambient field. It does not isolate the sensor from its surroundings,
so a strong or varying field nearby fails it exactly as a broken part would. What it does isolate is the
analogue front end from the digital path every other Diagnostics row exercises, and the size of the
failure tells the two apart: a few hundred counts out is a working coil buried in noise, while thousands
out with no coil response points at the part.

All five repeats must pass. A part returning noise lands inside the window by chance often enough that
one pass proves nothing.

## Diagnostics and the Compass page say which part is in use

A new Field from row names the part and carries its own counters, so the Data row above is not misread as
describing the magnetometer when it describes the accelerometer's transport. With a QMC5883L it shows the
overflow count, which means the range is too small rather than the sensor being noisy. With a BNO055 it
shows the four calibration figures and whether the external crystal was selected.

The Compass page no longer offers what it cannot do. With a self-calibrating part there is no sweep, so it
explains that instead of reporting a missing magnetometer, and its Calibration row reads `self, mag n/3`.

## What is still broken

The heading is suppressed and this release does not fix it. A stationary magnetometer reads with 200 to
400 µT of spread on every axis against an earth field of 25 to 65 µT, and the firmware refuses to publish
a bearing from that rather than render nonsense as a heading. Roll and pitch come from the accelerometer
and are unaffected.

Eliminated by measurement, so nobody needs to repeat it: the access path, the bus speed at 400 and
100 kHz, the auxiliary master's poll rate, byte order, the register map, synchronising the read to the
ICM's sample boundary, backlight PWM interference, accelerometer and gyroscope activity, and the
calibration and heading maths. Three modules from two suppliers behaved identically.

The one clue not yet chased is that the spread changes with the sample rate, which is what aliasing looks
like: a disturbance faster than the 100 Hz sample rate folding into the samples. Every remaining candidate
is hardware.

[Compass handoff](https://giovi321.github.io/had-badge-mod/development/compass-handoff/) is the full
account, including the three hardware tests worth running and the order to work in when a replacement
module arrives.

## Corrections to v1.0.0's documentation

Two statements in the v1.0.0 docs were wrong and are fixed:

- The magnetometer was described as reached through bypass mode as though that were correct. It was the
  cause of the bad readings.
- The self-test was described as independent of its environment. It is not. It measures the on-die coil
  plus the ambient field, and that error is what made a working part look dead.

## Verification status

Verified on hardware: the ICM-20948 is detected and identified, the AK09916 answers over the auxiliary
master, accepts a soft reset, enters continuous mode and streams into the shadow registers, and its
self-test runs. The accelerometer reads a steady 1.0 g, so roll and pitch are good. The firmware builds
clean and the portable suite passes 1135 checks.

Not verified: any heading against a known bearing, the QMC5883L driver, the BNO055 driver, and the axis
mapping for either new part. The compass remains beta and the heading is deliberately withheld.
